- **FEAT-PROCEDURAL-CV-BABBLE-VOC-001** — Procedural CV-babble vocal synth — Animal-Crossing-style speech-like utterances per emotion slot _(implemented)_
  Each bomber emits a short SPEECH-LIKE utterance at five emotional moments (place-bomb, hit-survived, pickup, death, victory). NOT a single grunt — a multi-syllable phrase that the listener parses as 'this character is talking', the way Animal Crossing's animalese reads as speech even though no real words exist. Each utterance is 2–5 syllables, each syllable is a consonant transient (10–30 ms) + a formant-shaped vowel pulse (40–100 ms), scheduled via Web Audio with an inter-syllable gap (10–40 ms) that produces conversational cadence. Per-bomber voice colour (10 seed-derived knobs) gives identity; per-slot phrase tables give emotion. Zero audio assets — pure synthesis. Supersedes the single-envelope direction in `docs/research/voice-synth-research.md` per the design override in `docs/game-design/voice-synth-design.md`. Rationale: User feedback 2026-05-22 explicitly rejected the single-grunt direction (paraphrased: the user wants the sounds the characters emit to feel like actual words, similar to Animal Crossing's animalese — where the player almost recognises speech without any real words being said). The dev research artifact (`docs/research/voice-synth-research.md`) correctly identified source-filter formant synth as the per-vowel primitive but capped scope at a single envelope per slot — that produces 'this robot beeped', not 'this character spoke'. Multi-syllable cadence is what triggers the speech-recognition heuristic in the listener's brain; spectral content alone doesn't. This story keeps every technical recommendation of the dev research that wasn't about utterance length and overrides only the multi-syllable exclusion. Full design rationale + reference matrix (8 known approaches, 5 rejected with reasons) lives in `docs/game-design/voice-synth-design.md`. - **BAL-WALK-ANIMATION-PHASE-MUS-001** — Walk-animation phase must advance with bomber velocity, not wall-clock _(implemented)_
  Shipped walk-swing animation in `bench-animation-system.ts` and consumed by the production `kaboom.bomber-animation-driver` advances its sine-wave phase off `BenchAnimationState.elapsed` — pure wall-clock. Result: a bomber on easy-difficulty preset (GridMover.speed = 2) animates at the SAME stride frequency as a hard-difficulty bomber (speed = 4). Two bombers with different Speed-Up power-up counts also stride identically. This breaks the readability link between movement speed and visual motion: the player can't see 'this bot moves slower than me' from the animation alone. The animation phase must be locked to TRAVELLED DISTANCE per fixedUpdate (Δphase = Δposition_magnitude × strideFrequencyPerCell), so slow bombers visibly take slow strides and fast bombers visibly hustle. Pure presentation fix — zero gameplay impact, zero performance cost. This was specified in GDP-2026-05-20-009 acceptance hints AND in GDP-2026-05-22-002 acceptance hints ('Phase advances with travelled distance, NOT wall-clock'), but slipped through both implementations. Rationale: Sources: `docs/game-design/characters-and-visual.md` §3.3 (the walk-cycle bullet — 'Phase advances with movement velocity, not wall clock — slow-walking bot animates slowly'), GDP-2026-05-20-009 acceptance hints (same line), GDP-2026-05-22-002 acceptance hints (same line). The constraint was always part of the design but never landed in code; this story is its corrective ticket. Locking walk phase to velocity is the single most important readability connection between gameplay state (BomberStats.speed, Speed-Up pickups, difficulty preset) and the on-screen body — without it, players can't read 'I'm faster than them' from looking. Acceptance hints (game-design proposal — dev to tighten into verification[]): - Investigation entry point: `examples/procbomber-bench/src/systems/bench-animation-system.ts` lines 81–125 — every walk-related pure helper (walkSwingX, walkSwingKneeElbow, walkRootBobY) currently uses `elapsed` directly. `examples/kaboom-crew/src/systems/bomber-animation-driver.ts` decides walk-swing kind but doesn't override the phase source. - DESIGN INTENT: walk-swing phase = ∫(velocity × strideFrequencyPerCell) dt. When velocity = 0, phase HOLDS (does not advance). When velocity = 1 cell/sec, phase advances at strideFrequencyPerCell × 2π radians per second. Default strideFrequencyPerCell = ~0.8 strides per cell (one full step covers ~1.25 cells of travel — feels natural at toy scale). - RECOMMENDED IMPLEMENTATION SHAPE (dev's call on exact factoring): - Option A — extend BenchAnimationState with a separate `walkPhase: number` field. Bench-only contexts that don't have a GridMover keep wall-clock fallback: walkPhase += dt × DEFAULT_FALLBACK_FREQ. - Option B — keep walkSwingX(elapsed, ...) signature; driver overwrites BenchAnimationState.elapsed each fixedUpdate to be a velocity-integrated value instead of wall-clock. Less clean (overloads the meaning of `elapsed`) but minimal diff. - Recommend Option A. Player's stride speed scales with collected Speed-Up power-ups in the same way. - A bomber stopped at a wall (collision keeps velocity_magnitude ≈ 0) → walk-cycle FREEZES at its current phase, doesn't continue swinging in place. - A bomber starting to move from rest → walk-cycle starts smoothly from its frozen phase, no snap. - UNIT TESTS: - walkSwingX(phase=0) → 0 (rest pose). - walkSwingX(phase=π) → opposite-sign extreme (one stride forward). - Phase integration: 10 ticks at dt=1/60 with velocity_magnitude = 2 → phase = 2 × 0.8 × 10/60 × 2π = ~1.676 rad.
